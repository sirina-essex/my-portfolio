Literature Review: Distributed Architectures and IoT Security

This literature review investigates how the shift towards distributed architectures (Edge, Fog, Cloud) in modern IoT systems creates operational cybersecurity challenges. It critically evaluates how recent studies address control dispersion, detection latency, governance gaps, and auditability, proposing structured perspectives for future research.

Governance and Control Dispersion

  • Fragmented Control Points: Each node becomes a partial authority, weakening consistency (NIST SP 800-207).
  • Centralized vs Distributed: Cloud centralization causes latency; hybrid or decentralized models improve resilience but add complexity (Masip-Bruin et al., 2016; Murad & Rahimi, 2024).
  • Resilience Trade-offs: P2P and local autonomy improve redundancy but challenge policy coherence (Anthony, 2023).
  • Dynamic Orchestration: SDN and AI-based controls offer adaptability but reintroduce central points of failure (Kalyashina et al., 2024).

Detection Latency and Embedded Monitoring

  • Centralized vs Local Detection: Cloud-based IDS suffer from delay; embedded models improve speed but require resources (Bablu & Rashid, 2020; Cuadrillero Paredes et al., 2024).
  • Distributed Deep Learning: Pre-trained models show potential but are difficult to scale in constrained environments (Diro & Chilamkurti, 2018).
  • Proactive Detection: Weak signal analysis and profiling are promising but unstable without standards (Gkonis et al., 2023).
  • Reactive vs Predictive: Reliable local execution competes with predictive fragility in Edge/Fog contexts (Ahmad et al., 2023).

Key Limitations

Many proposed solutions overestimate Edge/Fog capabilities and lack real-world validation. Issues include: unrealistic assumptions about node resources, poorly defined coordination schemes, lack of interoperable standards, and reintroduction of central points through SDN or Cloud orchestrators. Without embedded auditability and synchronised decision-making, security claims remain theoretical.

Perspectives and Research Priorities

  • Adaptive Orchestration: Dynamically assigning roles based on context (bandwidth, energy, risk).
  • Federated Governance: Flexible delegation of authority across nodes with formal identity and access policies.
  • Distributed Auditability: Non-repudiation through lightweight distributed ledgers or consensus protocols.
  • Federated Learning: Secure model training without sharing raw data, mitigating privacy risks.

These approaches must reconcile performance with resilience, coherence and traceability under severe constraints.

Conclusion

Securing distributed IoT systems demands more than decentralizing control—it requires integrated models that balance operational responsiveness, policy coherence and forensic auditability. Current frameworks lack full maturity, but emerging hybrid approaches combining Zero Trust, embedded intelligence and federated logic offer a promising way forward.
