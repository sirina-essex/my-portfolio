Executive summary

Executive summary of the collaborative exchanges held as part of the discussion on “Accuracy of Information” It highlights the key points raised by peers, the recurring themes across contributions, and the reflections that emerged from my interactions with others.

Summary Post

In my initial post, I explored the ethical issues surrounding Abi’s case, a statistical analyst facing a dilemma about nutritional data related to the new product Whizzz. Although there is no explicit falsification involved, Abi is considering alternative statistical analyses to present more favourable outcomes, despite clear indications of potential harm. I argued that such an approach constitutes an ethical lapse, amounting to questionable research practices (QRPs), particularly through cherry-picking and selectively manipulating results (Andrade, 2021; Ravn & Sorensen, 2021).

I emphasised that Abi's responsibility extends beyond merely technical reporting. Given the reasonable foreseeability of misuse of his findings, Abi must clearly document potential risks and maintain full transparency regarding all conducted analyses (Rao et al., 2024; Schatman & Thoman, 2014). I also highlighted the lack of a specific legal framework, thereby reinforcing the necessity to rely on fundamental principles such as transparency, integrity, and accountability, referencing notably the Declaration of Helsinki (WMA, 2013) and the Codex Alimentarius (FAO/WHO, 2023).

My peers have enriched the debate by providing several complementary and sometimes divergent perspectives:

Noora Alboinin (2025) and Ali Alzahmi (2025) strongly advocate for complete transparency and active engagement in addressing potential misuse of results, even in the absence of explicit legal obligations. They underline the importance of heightened vigilance, especially when a product might be consumed by vulnerable populations such as children (Hofseth, 2018; Sand et al., 2022; Codex Alimentarius, 2023).

Rodrigo Pereira Cruz (2025) aligns with this view, stressing Abi’s inability to maintain neutrality and independence due to commercial pressures, asserting that silence would be profoundly unethical and damaging to public trust (Slotfeldt-Ellingsen, 2023; Harlos, 2016).

Conversely, Kingsley Onyeemeosi (2025) questions the direct applicability of the Declaration of Helsinki, which is more suited to medical contexts rather than food products. He recommends instead explicitly using specialised guidelines such as the Ethical Guidelines for Statistical Practice (2023) and the Codex Alimentarius Procedural Manual (2025). Additionally, he brings a pragmatic perspective, proposing concrete measures such as explicitly including disclosure clauses in reports and advocating for better exploration of legal protections regarding whistleblowing.

Thus, this discussion clearly underscores that Abi cannot adopt a passive or strictly technical stance. The ethical, social, and professional issues require full transparency, absolute integrity in communicating findings, and ongoing vigilance concerning potential risks associated with widely consumed products.
