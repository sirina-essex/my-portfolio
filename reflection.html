Reflections

I have reflected of what I have learned, unit by unit, in Research Methods: the context, the actions, the result and my personal learning.

Introduction to to Research Methods - The Scientific Investigation and Ethics in Computing

Context: The unit introduced core concepts of the scientific method and research ethics in computing, focusing on inductive vs deductive reasoning and ethical responsibilities.

Actions: I explored the difference between reasoning types and reviewed key ethical principles from the Menlo Report and the BCS Code of Conduct.

Result: I understood when each reasoning method is appropriate and recognised ethics as a structural necessity in research involving people or sensitive data.

Learning: This unit strengthened my understanding of research logic and highlighted how ethical frameworks ensure responsible and credible research. I now see ethics not as a constraint, but as a foundation for quality research.

Research Questions, the Literature Review and the Research Proposal

Learning: This unit deepened my critical thinking and helped me view literature reviews as strategic tools to position my research within the academic field. It clarified how a well-structured question drives the entire project.

Context: This unit focused on defining a clear research topic, formulating research questions, and structuring a research proposal, with emphasis on the role of the literature review.

Actions: I explored techniques for narrowing research ideas into precise questions and reviewed methods for identifying, critiquing, and presenting academic literature.

Result: I learned how to transform a broad topic into a coherent proposal and how to use literature not just for background, but to identify gaps and justify research direction.

Methodology and Research Methods

Context: This unit introduced research paradigms, the distinction between methodology and methods, and the importance of aligning design choices with philosophical assumptions.

Actions: I explored ontological, epistemological, and axiological positions, and reviewed qualitative, quantitative, and mixed method approaches in relation to research design.

Result: I understood how assumptions shape research strategies and how methodological coherence supports validity and clarity in a project.

Learning: This unit helped me connect abstract research philosophy to practical design choices. I now approach methodology not as a formality, but as a lens that frames the entire research process.

Case Studies, Focus Groups and Observations

Learning: This unit sharpened my ability to evaluate qualitative tools critically. It taught me to be cautious about validity while appreciating the depth of insight these methods can provide when used appropriately.

Context: This unit focused on qualitative data collection methods: case studies, focus groups, and observations; and their respective strengths and limitations.

Actions: I analysed how each method gathers data, considered their suitability for different research contexts, and reflected on the risks of bias and limited generalisability.

Result: I understood the importance of selecting methods that match research objectives, and how data richness must be balanced against issues like researcher influence and sample representativeness.

Interviews, Survey Methods, and Questionnaire Design

Context: The unit explored how to design and apply interviews, surveys, and questionnaires to collect reliable qualitative or quantitative data.

Actions: I reviewed the logic behind question design, sampling, and pre-/post-testing, and analysed how different formats impact data quality and bias.

Result: I understood the importance of precision in question wording and structure, and how response types affect data interpretation.

Learning: Although I quickly realised that these techniques won’t directly apply to my own research project, I was still curious to understand them. It also allowed me to revisit tools I had encountered in my professional experience and to deepen my foundational knowledge with a more critical perspective

Quantitative Methods: Descriptive and Inferential Statistics

Learning: This unit allowed me to revisit concepts I had already encountered in other contexts and to deepen my technical grasp of statistical thinking. It clarified how these methods contribute to rigorous, methodologically sound research, an area I feel more confident navigating now.

Context: This unit introduced the fundamentals of quantitative analysis, focusing on levels of measurement, measures of location and dispersion, and descriptive statistics.

Actions: I reviewed how to summarise numerical data through key indicators (mean, median, standard deviation) and explored their role in identifying trends and variability..

Result: I reinforced my understanding of how statistical summaries support data-driven decision-making and prepare the ground for further analysis.

Inferential Statistics and Hypothesis Testing

Context: This unit focused on inferential statistics, introducing the logic of sampling, probability distributions, and hypothesis testing.

Actions: I studied how statistical inference allows researchers to generalise from sample data, and how to formulate and test hypotheses using appropriate methods.

Result: I understood the role of uncertainty and probability in data analysis, and how hypothesis testing helps validate or challenge assumptions with statistical rigour.

Learning: This unit helped me better grasp the logic behind inference and its practical applications. It was a good opportunity to consolidate concepts I had encountered before and to refine my understanding of how conclusions can be drawn responsibly from limited data.

Data Analysis and Visualisation

Learning: This unit reminded me that presenting data is not just about aesthetics, it’s about making results understandable and meaningful. It also helped me connect previous knowledge of business dashboards to a more structured research context.

Context: This unit explored how to analyse and present both quantitative and qualitative data, with an emphasis on coding, interpretation, and data visualisation tools like dashboards.

Actions: I reviewed coding techniques for qualitative data, the differences in analysis logic between data types, and how visual tools can support clear, actionable insights.

Result: I understood how thoughtful analysis and effective visualisation are crucial to communicate research findings, regardless of the data type.

Validity and Generalisability in Research

Context: This unit addressed key concepts that affect the credibility of research: validity, reliability, and generalisability, across both qualitative and quantitative designs.

Actions: I explored how these concepts influence research design and interpretation, and how data preparation (cleansing, validation) supports meaningful conclusions.

Result: I gained a clearer understanding of the conditions required for trustworthy results and the differences in how validity is approached depending on the data type.

Learning: This unit helped me critically reassess how I justify my methodological choices. It strengthened my awareness that research quality depends not only on tools, but on how well those tools fit the research question and context.

Research Writing

Learning: This unit gave me a clearer view of what is expected in academic research writing. It helped me shift from a purely technical mindset to one where articulation, justification, and flow are just as essential as content.

Context: This unit focused on how to structure and present academic research, particularly dissertations and research proposals, in a clear and rigorous way.

Actions: I examined the components of academic writing, including how to organise arguments, cite sources, and maintain coherence throughout a long research document.

Result: I better understood how structure supports clarity, and how each section of a dissertation contributes to building a convincing narrative.

Professional Development, e-Portfolio and Project Risk Management

Learning: Bringing together reflection and project control gave me a holistic view of my strengths and future challenges. It helped me realise how crucial it is to approach research not just as a technical task, but as a structured, evolving process that requires planning, adaptability, and continuous learning.

Context: These final units focused on professional growth through reflective practice, and on developing practical skills in project management, including risk and change control.

Actions: I reviewed my learning journey using the skills matrix and learning loop, and explored key project management principles such as lifecycle models, risk assessment, and change management.

Result: I gained a better understanding of how to manage uncertainty and structure projects effectively, while also identifying transferable skills and areas for ongoing development.
